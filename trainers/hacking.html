Assumes a healthy, fully powered target. Hack effects take five seconds to arrive.

Once a grid finishes, the grey bar briefly fills and then drains over five seconds, just as in Bridge Command. The hack takes effect when it reaches the bottom. Each successful grid applies half the displayed progress to the enemy system, after that five-second delay. Natural recovery removes 100 hacking points over 180 seconds. Effectiveness assumes a healthy system with 100% power, energy and no repair crew.

Source-accuracy note: From BCsrcNEW.zip: mineSweeper.cpp, miniGame.cpp, hackingDialog.cpp. This replica follows the source board sizes, mine density, adjacent clues, zero flood-fill, two-hit ending, Apply availability and the exponential progress display. The five-second transmission delay and the corresponding boost to the simulated target system run in real time. Optional instructor hints use only visible clues and are hidden by default. Natural target recovery is modelled; ship-specific repair crews, overheat, power changes and scanner heat from a successful hack are not included. The 70% threshold differs from the difficulty thresholds printed in the Academy manual; see explanation below.
Why does Apply unlock at about 60% displayed progress?

The code requires getProgress() > 0.7 + difficulty / 10. Both difficulty and 10 are integers, so for standard difficulties 0–3 the second term is zero. This makes the raw safe-cell threshold greater than 70% for every standard tier. Meanwhile, the progress indicator displays exp(rawProgress²) / exp(1), which reaches about 60% when raw progress reaches 70%. An automatic end after two routine hits is considered a successful partial breach by the C++ if raw progress exceeds 50%; otherwise it is a failed breach. This trainer reports both outcomes rather than assuming any mine hit means automatic failure.

How does the enemy system recover?

Per spaceship.cpp, natural hacking recovery is hacked_level -= delta / 180 until zero. Successful hack effects arrive five seconds after the grid ends and add 0.5 × exp(rawProgress² − 1) to the existing hacking level (capped at 100%). In the healthy, powered system shown here, effectiveness is 100% − 75% × hackedLevel. On an actual ship, damage, heat, power allocation, low energy and repair crews can change the observed outcome. Each successful hack also adds Scanner heat to the attacking ship; that heat is not simulated.
