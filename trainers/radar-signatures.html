What the trainer can and cannot teach: The trainer calculates physical signatures from each simulated ship's warp factor, jump delay, system heat, coolant and power settings using the C++ getDynamicRadarSignatureInfo() formula, rather than manually assigning colour amplitudes. It then recreates the 512-bin circular overlay, per-bin random noise, range attenuation, five-point smoothing and radial displacement from rawScannerDataRadarOverlay.cpp. The example ship states are invented training situations, but the emitted signals are calculated using the game code. This is not a live feed from a Bridge Command server.
How the Combined Signatures exercise worksFirst observe a quiet ship for 1.3 seconds. Its activity then changes, sometimes in two or three signature bands at once. Select the bearing and every band with a substantial new contribution, then submit the report. The exercise reveals an example of the activity producing those signatures (such as warp plus overheated systems). Replay returns to the quiet baseline so officers can compare the changes. The timer includes replay time.
Source and limitationsVerified against BCsrcNEW.zip: spaceObjects/spaceship.cpp (getDynamicRadarSignatureInfo) and screenComponents/rawScannerDataRadarOverlay.cpp. These are single-contact exercises rather than full simulation of every ship system; they do not reconstruct the navigation map or prove IFF, ship class, jump versus warp, or exact system power. For training clarity, bearings use 0° at the top and scenarios are placed sufficiently close for signatures to be distinguishable. Their apparent angular width is derived from ship radius and distance as in the source. The source clamps each accumulated signal to 1.0 before drawing it, so even Warp 1 typically saturates the red gravitational return at close range. One contact is simulated at a time; other mission objects and real network data are not included.
